Reactivation biology (steel-manning the hypothesis)

Defaults are deliberately generous to the disruption hypothesis (high recent-infection fraction and hazard). Push them all to the max and watch what it takes to reach the observed excess — and remember it still leaves the 2020 dip and Japan unexplained.
Observed rates: CDC (US), PHAC (Canada), UKHSA (England), Japan TB Surveillance Center, 2019–2024 (compiled). Model defaults: Canada LTBI ≈ 1.5 million persons (CMAJ 2018); remote reactivation ≈ 0.1%/yr and recent-infection risk ≈ 2–5%/yr over the first 2 years (Houben & Dodd, PLoS Med 2016); treatment reduces progression ≈ 69–93%. Completion defaults are empirical: in the 7-year Ottawa Hospital TB-clinic cascade, 66.1% of those who started LTBI treatment completed it, falling to 45.2% when care was transferred to primary care — a real-world analog of pathway disruption (BMC Public Health 2021). Note LTBI is not reportable in Canada, so the national annual-initiations count is the least-certain input — which is why the conclusion is stated as an upper bound. This is a transparent order-of-magnitude tool for arguing about mechanism, not a surveillance estimate. All parameters are user-adjustable.
